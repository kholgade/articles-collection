## What Level AI User Are You?

Steve Yegge is a famous American programmer.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026012408.webp)

He's worked at both Amazon and Google, but he's famous not for writing software, but for writing blogs.

He likes to publish long-form rants on his personal website — speaking his mind, being blunt, holding nothing back. Many of his articles are widely read in the industry and have caused quite a stir.

These articles were later compiled into a book, which was even translated and released in China under the title "Programmer's Roar" (Posts & Telecom Press, 2014).

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026012409.webp)

The English word in the original title is "ranting," which literally translates to "roaring" — and that's exactly his writing style.

This month, he published another [article](https://steve-yegge.medium.com/welcome-to-gas-town-4f25ee16dd04) about his views on AI programming.

**He says there are 8 levels of AI programming, and he's already reached Level 8 — the highest level.**

**Level 1**: You haven't encountered AI programming yet. Your IDE still looks normal (see below).

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026012410.webp)

**Level 2**: You've installed an AI plugin in your IDE and opened the sidebar. AI occasionally suggests code and asks if you accept it (Yes or No).

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026012411.webp)

**Level 3**: You start trusting AI coding and enter YOLO mode ("You Only Live Once"). To save time and effort, you no longer confirm each AI suggestion — you just hit Yes on everything AI generates and accept it all.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026012412.webp)

**Level 4**: The AI occupies an increasingly large portion of the screen width. The manual code editing area is only used for comparing code differences.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026012413.webp)

**Level 5**: You abandon the code area altogether and switch to the command line (e.g., Claude Code). All screen width is now given to AI. You no longer look at AI's generated output — you only check its progress.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026012414.webp)

**Level 6**: You find using just one AI too slow, so you open 3 to 5 windows and do AI programming simultaneously to speed things up.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026012415.webp)

**Level 7**: You have more than 10 AI coding windows open at once — the limit of what you can manually manage.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026012416.webp)

**Level 8**: You start using an AI task orchestrator, letting the computer manage multiple parallel AI coding sessions.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026012417.webp)

Those are the 8 levels of AI programming. What level are you?

But wait, it doesn't end there. As mentioned earlier, Steve Yegge himself has reached Level 8. He needed a tool to manage parallel AI coding sessions, but couldn't find a satisfactory one.

So, he had AI write one, and named it "Gas Town." The name comes from the fourth Mad Max movie — it's the lair of the main villain, Immortan Joe. The place is full of gas engines made from spare parts that barely work, constantly threatening to fall apart.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026012418.webp)

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026012419.webp)

He says the development of "Gas Town" was a patchwork affair — no consideration for reasonableness, just add whatever works, accept whatever doesn't throw an error. "It has 225,000 lines of Go code. I've never looked at its code, and I never plan to."

He advises users not to use this tool, because using it requires complete trust in AI. And even if you trust it, it might still make a complete mess of things. Plus, running multiple AIs simultaneously is expensive.

But he still put the [tool](https://github.com/steveyegge/gastown) online, because it's very fun. As of last week, it had already received 6,000 stars.

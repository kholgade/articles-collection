## Is Musk Afraid of Chinese Automakers?

Two weeks ago, Musk [announced](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/28/tesla-ending-model-s-x-production.html) that Tesla would stop production of the Model S and Model X.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202602/bg2026020710.webp)

His explanation was that the production lines would be freed up to manufacture robots and self-driving taxis.

This clearly deals a blow to Tesla's automotive business. Its consumer car lineup only had four models, and now two of them — the two most expensive ones — are gone, leaving only the low-priced Model 3 and Model Y.

At the press conference, a reporter asked whether there were plans to introduce new models.

Tesla's VP of Engineering responded:

> "You need to change how you think of us. We're now more about providing transportation services, not just offering vehicles for purchase."

Musk added:

> "I believe that in the long term, we will only produce autonomous vehicles."

This means Tesla has changed its positioning — **it will be a transportation company, not an automaker anymore**. The remaining two models could also be abandoned in the future; perhaps it will only produce self-driving vehicles that don't need drivers.

A foreign commentator immediately published a [sharp critique](https://electrek.co/2026/01/29/tesla-committing-automotive-suicide/), arguing that Tesla is giving up on car manufacturing — an act of suicide.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026013110.webp)

He said that Musk's interests have shifted elsewhere and he no longer persists in building cars. This is very regrettable and very foolish.

> "Tesla is allowing its very successful car manufacturing business to decline in order to pursue robots and self-driving taxis — things whose success, approval, and market demand are all uncertain."

He called on Musk not to fear competing with Chinese automakers, to persevere, and to build good and affordable American electric vehicles.

> "There could be another version of the future: Tesla remains a leader in the EV space while actively developing AI and autonomous driving technology, launching affordable models to compete with Chinese EVs and expanding into new market segments."

This seems to be a fairly common [view](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260205A0252T00): **Musk has lost confidence in competing with Chinese automakers and doesn't really want to continue building cars anymore**.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202602/bg2026020711.webp)

After all, Tesla's car sales declined in 2025 compared to 2024, and the title of "world's largest EV manufacturer" was ceded to BYD.

Is Musk afraid of Chinese automakers? Let me share my thoughts.

First, I don't know whether converting production lines to make robots is the right decision. I don't know if he still wants to build cars. All I know is that those criticizing him don't understand Musk at all.

**Never judge Musk by ordinary people's standards. He is not a conventional person.**

If you've read his biography, you'll know that all his entrepreneurial projects have been unconventional. From the early days of PayPal, to electric cars, solar power, reusable rockets, Starlink, brain-computer interfaces — they all have one thing in common: when he started them, there were no successful precedents, and therefore no competitors.

**Throughout his life, Musk has only enjoyed doing things that are "from 0 to 1" — cutting-edge things that no one has ever succeeded at before.** Making products similar to others and competing for market share is completely not his style.

Electric cars are already a mature commodity. The major technical problems have been solved. There are no barriers to entry. Everyone is now competing on cost. Do you think Musk would still be interested?

Let me tell you: he has no interest in beating Chinese automakers to become the world's largest and most advanced EV manufacturer. He doesn't need to make more money — he's already the richest person in the world.

On the other hand, building all-powerful robots, assembling a self-driving fleet, or engaging in other cooler, unprecedented ventures — that's much more Musk's style.

I think this is worth learning from. Chinese companies should not only pursue being bigger and stronger, but can also strive to do things never done before — not just being number one, but being the only one.

## Why Are Software Stocks Falling?

As you may know, in the last two or three years, due to the emergence of generative AI, the US stock market has soared.

All AI-related companies have seen their stock prices skyrocket: model companies, application companies, chip companies, storage companies...

However, I recently read a [news article](https://archive.is/2026.02.02-161143/https://www.economist.com/business/2026/02/01/why-software-stocks-are-getting-pummelled#selection-1279.183-1279.190) and learned that there's one category of stocks that not only didn't rise, but actually fell. You'd never guess — **these unlucky stocks are software stocks**.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202602/bg2026020404.webp)

The article states:

> "On January 29, SAP announced that its cloud business would experience slower growth, and its stock price plummeted 15%. Affected by this, other software stocks such as ServiceNow fell 13%, Salesforce 7%, and Workday 8%.
>
> This reflects growing anxiety about the future of the software industry. The industry experienced rapid growth during the pandemic, but has since slowed sharply. **Over the past year, publicly traded enterprise software companies in the US have fallen 10% overall**."

The article also included a stock price trend chart.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202602/bg2026020405.webp)

In the chart above, the upward black line is the broader market, and the downward colored lines are software stocks. The decline is truly appalling.

After reading the article, my first reaction was: these are US software stocks — what about Chinese software stocks?

I looked up [China's top 10 enterprise software stocks](https://m.thepaper.cn/baijiahao_5016224): China Software, Yonyou Network, Jiuqi Software, Inspur Software, SuperMap Software...

You can check the stock prices yourselves. Among these 10 companies, not a single one has outperformed the broader market in the past year. All have either declined or remained flat.

So I came to this conclusion: **the downturn in software stocks appears to be a global phenomenon**, regardless of country. Software companies' businesses are not looking optimistic.

Why is this? AI is surging ahead, continuously rising, while software stocks keep falling? Isn't AI part of software?

The answer is: **these listed software stocks are all enterprise software vendors** that have been public for many years, with products that were established before the advent of AI.

AI is not a boost to these software companies — it's a shock.

(1) AI enables companies to develop some of the software they need internally, reducing external purchases.

(2) AI-based software startups keep emerging, stealing business from existing software companies.

(3) AI can generate code quickly and continuously, so **code has become cheap**. This is the most important point. Software companies sell code, so they too have become cheap.

The three points above will not disappear in the future — they will only intensify. That's why people are not optimistic about software stocks.

However, there is uncertainty. There's the "[Jevons Paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jevons_paradox)," which states that when a resource's efficiency of use improves, its consumption doesn't decrease — it increases.

Software is exactly this case. AI improves the efficiency of software production, which will only lead the world to consume more software. Moreover, companies always need to purchase some software externally. The key question is: **will the new demand offset the reduction in traditional software procurement caused by AI?** If it can't, the future for software companies looks bleak.

Finally, there's one more point. It used to be said: "Talk is cheap, show me the code." Now, code is also cheap. You can't just look at the code — you need to see the running results.

1. [Birds Are More Afraid of Women Than of Men](https://nautil.us/birds-are-more-afraid-of-women-than-of-men-1280333?giftLink=ee36fe032c111e0fbf30fc9d314c006c&user_id=66c4c9305d78644b3ac5df9c)

A European team conducted a strange study: how close can a human get to a bird before it takes flight in alarm? This is called the "flight initiation distance."

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202605/bg2026050401.webp)

They tried to find people with similar hair length, body type, and height, and had them approach flocks of birds with the same gait. The result was that women startled birds into flight from a greater distance.

In other words, birds are more afraid of women than men. The team couldn't explain this result and proposed various hypotheses.

The most amusing hypothesis is that during evolution, men may have been responsible for hunting large prey, while women hunted smaller prey like birds. Thus, birds gradually evolved to be more afraid of women.

2. [Traffic Cameras with Bluetooth Recognition](https://www.404media.co/this-company-will-add-phone-airpod-and-smartwatch-trackers-to-license-plate-readers/)

A US startup has introduced a new type of traffic camera mounted on roads that not only recognizes license plates but also records the MAC addresses of Bluetooth devices.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202606/bg2026062009.webp)

Typically, traffic violations can only be recorded by license plate, not by identifying who is driving the vehicle.

This new camera solves that problem. It has an additional sensor that can detect Bluetooth signals, thereby linking a specific Bluetooth device (e.g., headphones) to the license plate, determining who is in the car.

In addition to Bluetooth, it can also recognize RFID tags in key cards and pet microchips, as well as the SSID of in-car Wi-Fi.

3. [Sound Wave Fire Extinguishing](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/05/startup-says-sound-waves-can-replace-fire-sprinklers-experts-arent-so-sure/)

Besides water, sound waves can also be used to extinguish fires.

Physics has proven that strong low-frequency sound waves (30Hz–60Hz) can be used to put out fires. The principle is that sound waves can block oxygen inflow and accelerate heat dissipation.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202605/bg2026050402.webp)

A US startup recently built a sound wave fire extinguishing device and is preparing to bring it to market. They successfully demonstrated a kitchen fire extinguishing scenario.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202605/bg2026050403.webp)

The speaker blasts sound waves at the stove, and the fire goes out. The company hopes this device can replace sprinkler systems, so that in the future, if a fire breaks out, the speakers overhead can just play sound to put it out.

However, it has several drawbacks that may make it difficult to promote. First, the speaker is relatively large, otherwise the sound waves aren't strong enough. Also, it must be close to the fire source because sound waves attenuate quickly. This also means it can only be used for small, localized fires, not for large, widespread blazes.

5. [The Longest Line of Sight](https://earthlymission.com/longest-view-world/)

Scientists used algorithms to determine the longest line of sight on Earth—the greatest distance from which one location can see another.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202602/bg2026021618.webp)

That is from Pik Dankova in Kyrgyzstan (near the Chinese border) looking south, where the Kunlun Mountains deep in China can be clearly seen, at a distance of 530 kilometers.

Generally, the higher the altitude and the clearer the air, the farther you can see.

Pik Dankova is about 6,000 meters high. To the south lies the low-lying Qaidam Basin desert, and beyond that are the Kunlun Mountains, also over 6,000 meters high. Thus, these two high mountains hundreds of kilometers apart can see each other.

6. [Wooden Bases for Wind Turbines](https://www.bbc.com/news/science-environment-67718719)

To achieve greater power output, wind turbines are getting bigger and bigger.

Currently, wind turbine bases are cylindrical steel towers. As their diameter and height increase, transportation and erection become increasingly difficult.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202312/bg2023123009.jpeg)

A Swedish company recently proposed a new solution: using wooden boards to build wind turbine bases.

They glue and compress multiple layers of wood together to form curved segments. These segments are then taken to the construction site, glued into a cylinder, and stacked to form a tower.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202312/bg2023123010.webp)

The advantage is that the base is much lighter and can be installed in sections, making transportation and erection easier.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202312/bg2023123011.webp)

Looking inside the base, you can clearly see the wood grain on the walls.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202312/bg2023123012.webp)

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202312/bg2023123013.webp)

If this type of wood panel is proven to be suitable for wind turbine bases—with sufficient strength and fire resistance—then it could also be used to build high-rise buildings, offering broad prospects.

7. [How to Eliminate Fan Noise](https://uploader-worker.yifeng-ruan.workers.dev/)

Fans always make noise. What to do?

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202509/bg2025092008.webp)

A foreign programmer noticed that the fan's noise frequency is fixed (his was 312Hz). So he thought of borrowing the idea from noise-canceling headphones: using an opposite frequency to cancel the noise.

He placed a Bluetooth speaker near the fan, playing a 312Hz tone to cancel the noise by phase inversion.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202509/bg2025092009.webp)

Unfortunately, his setup was too crude to achieve precise phase alignment, so the effect was not obvious.

2. [Why Does Squinting Help You See Better?](https://www.mentalfloss.com/posts/why-does-squinting-help-you-see-better)

We've all experienced this: when you're having trouble seeing something far away, squinting improves clarity. Think about it, it's counterintuitive. Squinting reduces the usable part of your eye, so why does it make things clearer?

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202606/bg2026062510.webp)

You might think it has to do with eye pressure—squinting "squeezes the eyeball," temporarily reshaping the lens's focus to better focus. That idea is wrong.

The real effect of squinting is to reduce the amount of light entering the eye. By squinting, you effectively make the eye smaller, reducing interference from excess light, allowing only light near the center of your field of vision to enter, thereby concentrating the light on the retina. This is called the pinhole effect, and it's the fundamental reason for clearer vision.

The downside of squinting for your eyes is mainly that you blink less. So prolonged squinting (e.g., squinting at a computer screen all day) can lead to eye fatigue or dry eye.

Contrary to some opinions, squinting does not damage your vision, although frequent squinting can be a symptom of declining eyesight. If you find yourself squinting all the time, you need to get your eyes checked.

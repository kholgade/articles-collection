## Excerpt

1. [Three Constraints Before Building Anything](https://jordanlord.co.uk/blog/3-constraints/)

I've been in product development for 10 years and have developed some failed products—either too complex or lacking distinctive features. After making these mistakes, I summarized three constraints.

Since then, I use these three constraints before building anything. If a new project doesn't meet any of these constraints, I won't develop it.

(1) Write all your ideas into a one-page summary.

If a project's introduction needs more than one page to write, it's too complex—don't develop it.

(2) Core technology must be separated from the product.

Products constantly pivot, while core technology remains consistent and evolves continuously.

Separating core technology from the product forces you to think beyond the product itself.

(3) The product must have a core constraint.

This constraint is clear and gives your product a unique personality. For example, *Minecraft* is entirely made of blocks; IKEA offers flat-packed, self-assembled furniture.

The constraint you choose narrows down decision space, allowing you to focus on what truly matters. If you don't set a constraint, or choose the wrong one, you'll end up with a bloated product trying to do everything.

# How to View the "Largest Open-Source Model Ever" — Kimi K3?

## I

Everyone's been flooded with news about [Kimi K3](https://www.kimi.com/blog/kimi-k3) over the past couple of days. How should we view this largest-ever open-source model?

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202607/bg2026071702.webp)

Let me share my thoughts, broken down into several points.

**First, is its performance really close to Fable 5?**

Based on tests from multiple overseas organizations, as well as my own testing (included later), I believe yes.

Arena AI ranks it [first](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/code/webdev) in web development. Note that this only compares frontend programming; Fable 5 still leads in other areas.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202607/bg2026071706.webp)

Independent evaluation organization Artificial Analysis ranks it [third](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2077832874183860404).

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202607/bg2026071804.webp)

Other tests, such as office capability [GDPval v2](https://artificialanalysis.ai/#gdpval), writing ability [Writing Elo](https://x.com/Whats_AI/status/2077860441380798908), vibecoding [BridgeBench](https://x.com/bridgebench/status/2078109314074443846), and full-stack programming [Next.js](https://nextjs.org/evals) — all rank it in the top three, sometimes first.

I believe its self-positioning in the release announcement is credible: slightly inferior to Claude Fable 5 and GPT 5.6 Sol, but stronger than Claude Opus 4.8.

## II

**Why did its capability suddenly become so strong?**

Its previous version, Kimi K2.7 Code, was released on June 12 and performed only moderately, ranking just 17th in Artificial Analysis.

Why such a huge improvement in just one month?

Compared to previous versions, K3's biggest difference is the parameter count increasing from 1T to 2.8T — likely the reason for its capability leap.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202607/bg2026071701.webp)

This 2.8T parameter count is unprecedented, far exceeding other open-source models — enough to rival foreign flagships.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202607/bg2026071703.webp)

As we know, parameter count represents the factors a large model considers when computing tokens. More parameters mean more factors considered — generally leading to better generation results.

## III

**How does Kimi reduce computation?**

More parameters mean more computation, which increases usage cost and response time.

So the real challenge isn't adding parameters — that's just fitting more variables to equations — but controlling computation so it doesn't explode with parameter growth.

To reduce computation, Kimi K3 fully utilizes the Mixture of Experts (MoE) architecture. This architecture only activates a subset of parameters per operation, reducing computation. Kimi has further reduced the activation rate.

So far, they haven't published the number of activated parameters. The release report only states:

> "We have also extended the sparsity of the Mixture of Experts (MoE) model. When combined with the Stable LatentMoE framework, it can effectively activate 16 out of 896 experts. Combined with improved training and data processing methods, these structural changes increase overall scaling efficiency by approximately 2.5x compared to Kimi K2, enabling the model to more effectively convert computation into intelligence."

Based on this: Kimi K3 has 896 experts, equivalent to ~3.13B parameters per expert (2800/896). Activating 16 experts means activating ~50B parameters.

The report also says K3 is 2.5x more efficient than K2. K2 had 1T parameters activating 32B — a 3.2% efficiency. Dividing by 2.5 gives 1.28%, equivalent to 2.8T parameters activating ~36B.

Whether 50B or 36B, both indicate Kimi K3 is quite sparse, with an even smaller activation proportion — which is why the announcement mentions "extending the sparsity of the MoE."

Therefore, K3's actual computation isn't as large as it appears.

## IV

**Kimi also uses other methods to reduce computation**, beyond increasing parameter sparsity.

K3's context window has also increased from 256K to 1M, which would increase memory usage. They use a method called Kimi Delta Attention (KDA), which dynamically deletes some past information as new context is added, reducing memory usage.

Additionally, models have many layers. Computing every layer is computationally expensive. They use Attention Residuals technology to dynamically skip some layers, saving computation.

I'm not deeply familiar with these technical details. The key point is that through various methods, Kimi K3 significantly reduces computation, preventing the massive parameter scale from causing prohibitive costs.

## V

Even so, **Kimi K3 is still expensive to use**. You should set your expectations — this is a high-cost model.

Its domestic API pricing is 20 yuan / 100 yuan per million tokens for input/output — several times more than the previous version K2.7 Code (6.7 / 27 yuan), and much more expensive than other domestic models. It's probably the most expensive model in China right now.

Its overseas API pricing is $3 / $15, on par with Anthropic's Claude Sonnet series — the most expensive Chinese model sold overseas to date.

Due to the high pricing, each task costs quite a bit. According to Artificial Analysis, its per-task cost is $0.94, close to GPT 5.6 Sol ($1.04), but much cheaper than Claude models — about half of Claude Opus 4.8 ($1.80), let alone Claude Fable 5.

The good news is that per-task token consumption has decreased — K3 uses 21% fewer output tokens than K2.6, helping save costs.

## VI

Below is my test of Kimi K3. I referenced a [foreign review](https://www.tryai.dev/blog/gpt-5.6-build-off-12-models), gave Kimi K3 the same test questions, and directly compared with existing results.

I selected three top-tier foreign flagship models as comparison targets:

> - Claude Fable 5
> - GPT 5.6 Sol
> - Claude Opus 4.8

All test results are online — click the links to view.

**Test 1**: Generate a DOOM-style 3D maze with ceilings, floors, and brick walls in first-person view, controllable with WASD keys.

> [Prompt] First-person raycaster you walk with WASD, shaded walls with depth, floor and ceiling, collision.

[Kimi K3](https://ruanyf.github.io/ai-test-case/kimi-k3/case01/index.html)

Click through to compare — which do you think is best?

I'd rank GPT 5.6 first — closest to the original DOOM, adding a gun and crosshair, plus a mini-map in the lower right.

Kimi K3 ranks second — good maze feel, smooth operation. Fable 5 ranks third — very ordinary, with oddly narrow pathways that make you feel like your face is against the bricks. Opus 4.8, while also ordinary-looking, is actually better than Fable 5, but has a bug where certain intersections become unresponsive.

## VII

Through these four tests, Kimi K3's performance is fully comparable to those flagship models, with significant progress over its previous version. Increasing parameter count indeed helps model quality.

You might say these questions aren't difficult enough to test the model's true capability. But looking horizontally, Fable 5 isn't that impressive either — it didn't significantly outshine others.

Considering Kimi K3's price is less than a third of Fable 5's, with this performance plus open weights for free deployment — will this keep Anthropic founder Dario Amodei up at night? I hope so.

Kimi K3's 2.8T parameters are surely just the beginning, opening the curtain on Chinese open-source models moving toward larger scales. In the future, models with T-sized parameters will emerge in droves. As long as computing power keeps up, domestic open-source models catching up to and surpassing foreign flagship models is entirely possible.

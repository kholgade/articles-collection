## AI Open Source Is a False Proposition

Below are statements from Anthropic founder Dario Amodei, [shared online](https://www.threads.com/@whaleagent/post/DaJx5pfkzmL).

Let's see if you agree with his view, or think it's deliberately confusing and nonsense.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202606/bg2026063002.webp)

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202606/bg2026063003.webp)

He says that even if an AI model is made public, you still can't see its internal workings. That's why the industry doesn't call it "open source" but "open weights."

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202606/bg2026063004.webp)

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202606/bg2026063005.webp)

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202606/bg2026063006.webp)

With traditional open-source projects, many people can join development together, make modifications, pool collective wisdom, and the project keeps getting better.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202606/bg2026063007.webp)

But open-source AI models don't work this way. They just publish weight files — you can't submit modifications or participate in their development.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202606/bg2026063008.webp)

So when he sees a new model, he never asks whether it's open source — that's irrelevant. He only asks one question: is it good enough, and has it beaten us on important tasks?

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202606/bg2026063009.webp)

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202606/bg2026063010.webp)

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202606/bg2026063011.webp)

Moreover, an open-source model doesn't mean a free model. The model is still so large that you still have to pay to run it in the cloud, run inference, and have someone debug it to make it run fast enough.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202606/bg2026063012.webp)

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202606/bg2026063013.webp)

Rather than obsessing over whether it's open source, it's better to focus on who developed the model and whether it does what you need it to do best.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202606/bg2026063014.webp)

These are Dario Amodei's exact words. I think it's pure nonsense, spoken with eyes wide open.

Even if open-source models only open weights, can they be the same as closed-source models? Without needing anyone's permission, I can run an open-source model in the cloud — all conversations and data remain private. But can I run a Claude model myself?

What's more, who says you can't see the internal workings? DeepSeek publicly published [papers](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3401632759482502) explaining how their model operates. Claude models, on the other hand, are true black boxes.

Additionally, I received an email from Anthropic yesterday notifying me that my account has been suspended for violating their usage policy.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202606/bg2026063016.webp)

How ridiculous. My Anthropic account was opened through a third country (since they block Chinese IP addresses) when their model first launched, just for testing. I only used it twice — simple questions on the web — and hadn't touched it for at least three months.

I haven't done the slightest damage to their service. Yet they even ban my "semi-dormant account." The only reason is that I'm a Chinese user and they can't let any slip through.

This will never succeed — it will only backfire.

Let me put it this way: as far as I know, in all of human history, no technology has ever managed to keep its secrets from spreading. From [Chinese silk](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_10900070) and [British looms](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/281895895) in ancient times, to [nuclear technology](https://www.sohu.com/a/520520453_120934258) and [cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Export_of_cryptography_from_the_United_States) in modern times, the pattern holds. I believe that in the long run, large model technology and chip technology will be no exception.

Anthropic's blatant, discriminatory "Chinese not allowed" policy will only be counterproductive. The more they close off, the more they accelerate the development of open-source models, leaving themselves isolated and surrounded by open-source models.

(End)

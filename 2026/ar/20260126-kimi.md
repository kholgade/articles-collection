# Kimi's Integration, Manus's Layering

## I

The day before yesterday, Kimi suddenly released its [flagship model K2.5](https://www.kimi.com/blog/kimi-k2-5.html) without any prior notice.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026012802.webp)

In China, Kimi is a relatively low-key company with less attention. However, its products are not weak.

Six months ago, the K2 model made a stunning debut, receiving high praise and being widely recognized as belonging to the global first tier. So when the new version K2.5 came out, it immediately made the news, becoming a hot topic on platforms like Hacker News and Twitter.

Famous developer Simon Willison wrote a [detailed introduction](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jan/27/kimi-k25/) the same day.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026012803.webp)

However, what's truly interesting this time isn't the model itself, but something else Kimi did.

## II

This K2.5 is very powerful, with improvements over K2 in every aspect. The official benchmark scores place it among the top three globally, even first place in some categories (see the [release notes](https://www.kimi.com/blog/kimi-k2-5.html)).

According to the LMArena (now renamed arena.ai) [leaderboard](https://x.com/arena/status/2016294725813465114), Kimi K2.5's coding ability ranks first among all open-source models, second only to Claude and Gemini overall (see below).

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026012902.webp)

But the biggest highlight isn't actually the model itself — Kimi simultaneously released an Agent based on this model.

In other words, **two things were released at once this time: the K2.5 model and the K2.5 Agent**. K2.5 is the underlying model, while K2.5 Agent is a web application aimed at end users.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026012813.webp)

As far as I can recall, this seems to be the first time a major AI company has done this. Previously, releases were just the model itself; no one had bundled a model and Agent together before.

Simply put, Kimi has embarked on the path of integration.

## III

As everyone knows, large models are the underlying processing engine, while Agents are the user-facing applications built on top.

**Their relationship essentially comes in two forms: layered development and integrated development.** The former separates the large model and agent, developing them independently; the latter builds them together as a whole.

Not long ago, Manus — acquired by Meta at a high price — is the best example of layered development.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026012814.webp)

Manus uses Anthropic's Claude model, building an independent agent on top of it, and was eventually acquired.

Its success has inspired many to invest in agent development. Since model development requires enormous investment that not everyone can afford, agents require comparatively less investment, even small developers can build them.

Kimi's attempt this time takes a big step in the opposite direction, combining the large model and Agent into one. After all, large model companies find it more convenient to do this themselves, which helps expand market share and attract users.

It's hard to say which approach is better. It's like smartphones — Apple and Android's third-party apps can better meet user needs, while built-in apps integrate more smoothly with the operating system.

## IV

There have been plenty of model benchmark tests already, so below I'll test the newly released K2.5 Agent.

It's clear that Kimi places great importance on the Agent and has invested significant effort. The [release notes](https://www.kimi.com/blog/kimi-k2-5.html) devote most of their篇幅 to introducing the Agent's features.

Some features are relatively conventional:

> (1) **Kimi Office Agent**: Expert-level Word, Excel, and PowerPoint file generation.
> 
> (2) **Kimi Code**: A command-line tool comparable to Claude Code, specifically for code generation.
>
> (3) **Long-range operation**: Can complete up to 1500 steps in a single session, clearly benchmarking against Manus, which is known for its multi-step operations.

I'm particularly interested in the following two brand-new features, which I've seen for the first time and don't seem to have been mentioned by other companies.

> (4) **Visual Programming**: Using the model's visual capabilities to understand images and videos for programming. Simply upload design drafts and web videos, and it can generate the corresponding webpage.
>
> (5) **Swarm Function** (agent swarm): When encountering complex tasks, the Agent can internally call up to 100 agents to form a cluster and execute tasks concurrently, such as concurrent downloading or concurrent generation.

Given space constraints, I'll briefly share my "Visual Programming" test results.

## V

First, open the [Kimi website](https://www.kimi.com/). K2.5 is already online and can be used directly (see below).

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026012804.webp)

Note: Switch the model to "Agent Mode" K2.5 Agent.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026012805.webp)

My first test was generating motion effects — uploading a video of an animation effect and having it regenerate. The original animation was made with the [Lottie library](https://lottiefiles.com/free-animation/cat-playing-animation-1cwXJbHzz7).

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026012806.gif)

After uploading, I entered the prompt on the webpage:

> Recreate the animation effect from the video exactly as shown on the webpage.

The model quickly deduced that this was an animation of an orange cat playing with a ball. It then surprisingly took screenshots of each animation frame to reconstruct it.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026012807.webp)

Finally, it used Python to generate an SVG animation file.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202601/bg2026012809.webp)

The animation effects of the tail, eyeballs, and rolling ball were all correctly reproduced. Unfortunately, the main cat was composed of multiple SVG shapes, so it couldn't look very similar.

You can visit [this link](https://64iapat2s7a4k.beta-ok.kimi.link/) to see the final result and webpage code.

## VII

After brief testing, my assessment is that Kimi K2.5 Agent's "Visual Programming" is not just hype — it genuinely has visual understanding capabilities and can produce usable results.

Currently, it appears that Kimi's "Model + Agent" integrated approach is successful. On one hand, the powerful Agent brings out the underlying model's capabilities, making it convenient for users; on the other hand, the model extends its use cases through the Agent, attracting more users and benefiting its own promotion.

Finally, within the current landscape of international competition, integration has an additional advantage.

Manus relies on American models and ultimately had to register its company overseas, whereas Kimi's underlying model is self-developed and open-source, completely avoiding the risk of being cut off.

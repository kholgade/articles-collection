# A Shell Company Using Chinese LLMs to Support a $50 Billion Valuation? Unpacking Cursor's "Shell" Controversy

## 1

This weekend, the hottest AI news shook both sides of the Pacific, even catching Elon Musk's attention.

Yesterday, AI coding tool Cursor launched its "own" model, [Composer 2](https://cursor.com/cn/docs/models/cursor-composer-2).

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202603/bg2026032101.webp)

The screenshot above is from the official website, which still reads "own model."

Since Composer 1 was released in October 2024, outsiders have suspected it was a shell wrapped around a Chinese model, due to very similar behavior — but they couldn't find proof.

Now Composer 2 is here, and many are investigating what model is actually behind it — is it really Cursor's own?

Cursor implemented many restrictions to prevent reverse engineering, but one slipped through. Twitter user @fynnso [discovered](https://x.com/fynnso/status/2034710006546014363) a place that was blocked in the previous version but executable in this one.

First, set up your own server acting as an AI model call interface. It doesn't matter what model it uses — it just needs to receive client requests.

Then, in your local Cursor, set the model to Composer 2 with the model URL pointing to your newly set up server. This way, Cursor sends requests to your server, allowing you to see which model it's actually requesting.

The truth was exposed: the requested model ID was kimi-k2p5-rl-0317-s515-fast (shown below).

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202603/bg2026032102.webp)

## 2

The Twitter user posted the screenshot [online](https://x.com/fynnso/status/2034706304875602030). Pandemonium ensued — anyone with eyes could see this was solid evidence: **Composer 2 is actually a shell wrapped around Kimi K2.5**.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202603/bg2026032115.webp)

Ironically, as soon as the news broke, Cursor immediately patched the vulnerability — you can no longer reproduce this request (shown below).

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202603/bg2026032103.webp)

But it was too late — it spread everywhere. Even Elon Musk [tweeted](https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2034941631871455262): "It's Kimi K2.5."

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202603/bg2026032104.webp)

Now it's an open secret that can no longer be hidden.

## 3

Attention quickly shifted to whether Cursor had infringed copyright. Kimi K2.5 is open-source, but under a modified MIT license (shown below).

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202603/bg2026032105.webp)

The license states: you can use this model freely, with the only condition being that if your commercial product has over 100 million monthly active users or exceeds $20 million in monthly revenue, **you must disclose your use of Kimi K2.5 in a prominent location in your user interface**.

Cursor's latest disclosed annualized revenue is $2 billion, equivalent to approximately $167 million in monthly revenue — clearly meeting the above condition. Yet it concealed the fact that it was using K2.5.

Just as everyone was concluding that Cursor had infringed, one of their executives finally spoke up [in response](https://x.com/leerob/status/2035050444347600936).

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202603/bg2026032106.webp)

He acknowledged that they do use Kimi K2.5 but claimed no infringement, as their license came from their partner Fireworks AI.

Shortly after, Kimi's official account also [tweeted](https://x.com/Kimi_Moonshot/status/2035074972943831491).

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202603/bg2026032107.webp)

Kimi's official confirmation stated that Cursor obtained authorization from Fireworks AI, a Silicon Valley Chinese-founded AI company specializing in fine-tuning and reinforcement learning of AI models. Fireworks AI obtained authorization from Kimi to retrain the model, then sublicensed it to Cursor.

## 4

At this point, the situation is basically clear: Cursor did not violate Kimi's licensing terms, so there's no infringement.

If that's the case, why did they try so hard to conceal this fact? Wouldn't it have been easy to openly acknowledge providing a modified version of Kimi K2.5?

I suspect **the reason is related to Cursor's ballooning valuation**.

Bloomberg reported this month that Cursor is in its next funding round, with a valuation reaching $50 billion.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202603/bg2026032108.webp)

Do you know what its previous valuations were?

In October 2023, Cursor was founded with a $50 million valuation. In August 2024, its Series A round pushed the valuation to $400 million. By December, Series B quickly rose to $2.6 billion. The most recent round in November 2025 reached $29.3 billion.

Every few months, the valuation doubles. This rocket-like ascent needs performance to support it. But Cursor is essentially just a modified version of VS Code, using open-source technology.

To sustain its ever-increasing valuation, **it has the incentive to rebrand itself from an AI tool into an AI company with model R&D capabilities**.

I believe this is the main reason it was unwilling to disclose its use of Kimi K2.5.

## 5

Looking at the whole incident, Cursor is undoubtedly the loser, while Kimi is the winner — getting a massive wave of high-value exposure for free.

When Cursor launched Composer 2, it disclosed performance and cost comparisons.

Composer 2's performance is below GPT-5.4 but above Opus 4.6.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202603/bg2026032109.webp)

However, its generation speed is faster than both GPT-5.4 and Opus 4.6, with the lowest cost.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202603/bg2026032110.webp)

Since Composer 2 is essentially fine-tuned Kimi K2.5, **using Kimi directly would yield the same results**.

## 6

Previously, foreign critics always accused Chinese companies of stealing foreign technology. But this incident proves that Chinese companies also have technology to export. Those foreign star companies are secretly using Chinese technology behind the scenes.

This is reminiscent of last week, when Kimi's founder Yang Zhiping received an invitation from Jensen Huang to speak at Nvidia GTC, [the only representative from a Chinese AI company](https://www.51cto.com/article/838459.html).

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202603/bg2026032112.webp)

On stage, he presented the paper ["Attention Residuals"](https://github.com/MoonshotAI/Attention-Residuals) recently published by the Kimi team.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202603/bg2026032113.webp)

This new technique is said to significantly enhance the reasoning capabilities of large models.

My take is that we should have confidence in domestic large models — they are perfectly suitable for everyday work. The gap between domestic large models and foreign flagship models is narrowing, and they offer great value for money.

![](https://cdn.beekka.com/blogimg/asset/202603/bg2026032114.webp)

[According to Yang Zhiping](https://www.51cto.com/article/835315.html), the upcoming K3 model will have dramatically improved performance — even if not 10x stronger, it will be significantly better than K2.5. Something to look forward to.
